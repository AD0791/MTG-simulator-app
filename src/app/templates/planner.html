How it is worked out

Every {{ plan.style }} risks the same share of the balance as it stands, so wins and losses multiply and their order does not matter. With r the risk and w what a win adds:

daily = (target ÷ capital)1 ÷ D − 1 daily growth equals target divided by capital, to the power of one over D, minus one, where D is the number of trading days

{% if plan.style == "session" %}

w = r × (2 × payout − 1) ÷ 3  to  r × payout ÷ 3 a session win adds between r times two payout minus one, divided by three, when the recharge wins, and r times payout divided by three, when the initial trade wins

{% else %}

w = r × payout a win adds r times the payout

{% endif %}

wins = ⌈(ln(target ÷ capital) − N × ln(1 − r)) ÷ (ln(1 + w) − ln(1 − r))⌉ wins needed equals the ceiling of: the natural log of target over capital, minus N times the natural log of one minus r, all divided by the natural log of one plus w minus the natural log of one minus r, where N is every {{ plan.style }} in the range

break-even share = −ln(1 − r) ÷ (ln(1 + w) − ln(1 − r)) the break-even win share equals minus the natural log of one minus r, divided by the natural log of one plus w minus the natural log of one minus r

The target is out of reach when the wins needed exceed every {{ plan.style }} available; the planner then shows where winning all of them would end.

This states what the target requires. It does not estimate whether anyone wins that often. At a 92% payout, merely breaking even already needs about 52% of single trades and about 78% of sessions.
